**Fixed:** 25 October 2026 local time, after the six-cell utility/conditional results and before joint-implied results. Development assessment only; official Adult test and confirmation seeds404/505 remain locked.

Use the six fixed projected cells: Chow-Liu age/K5 and mixed-GMM label-skew/K10 at development seeds101/202/303. Keep their local models, alpha, fixed size/constrained weights, development-assessment people and row IDs unchanged. For each real feature row X, score both valid income labels under the released joint mixture and normalize their two scores to obtain the joint-implied income probability. Chow-Liu's projected integer/discrete score is exact for its declared fitted model. Mixed GMM's rounded/clipped mixed-data score is **approximate**, so its normalized ratio is an approximate predictive route, not an exact conditional probability of the emitted rows. Never compare the raw score magnitudes across model families.

If both label scores have zero or nonfinite joint mass, mark the feature row **off support** and abstain; never substitute a prior or kernel smoother. Report unweighted and alpha-standardized coverage, per-client coverage and the number of each off-support reason. Evaluate AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, log loss and fixed-bin ECE on covered rows only, with alpha-standardized client weights. Compare the previously trained/calibrated synthetic logistic and extra-trees predictors and privileged TRTR references on **exactly those same covered people**. Save row-level paired probabilities and prediction metrics in evaluator-only artifacts. For any client with one observed income class, discrimination metrics are undefined rather than imputed. No real held-out row enters joint fitting or method-weight selection.

The comparison is descriptive across three overlapping Adult split seeds and two selected generator/skew families. It does not demonstrate off-support performance, arbitrary conditional accuracy, privacy, or general superiority. Run under the existing shared lease with one native thread and retain failures.
