# Proof review — 31 October 2026

This is a post-experiment review of the 27 labeled statements in the report's theory chapters. The supplement keeps the report's label strings as stable IDs, uses a single sequence 1–27, and supplies the associated proofs or counterexamples. Standard arguments are not claimed as original. The frozen experimental method and results are unchanged.

## Findings and qualifications

- The report's parallel-privacy proof conditions on unaffected outputs; its stated independence of local randomization must mean independence across client mechanisms given the fixed datasets, with unaffected client mechanisms depending only on their own fixed data. The supplement makes the randomization qualifier explicit. Shared randomness, cross-client data dependence, or data-dependent membership require a separate joint/conditional privacy proof. The main release is not certified private.
- The Brier identity requires squared loss; the supplement specifies this globally. The probability and risk arguments require measurable functions, well-defined conditional distributions, and positive conditioning mass. The transport bound requires the displayed KL-to-product convention, finite supports of size at most B and nonnegative regularization; another entropy convention cannot be substituted without adjustment.
- The finite-workload bounds assume queries in [0,1], independence of fresh generated rows, and conditioning on the selected weights and fixed models. Data-adaptive query discovery or repeatedly selected pilots require an additional uniform or independent-data argument. The baseline and candidate tables in the fallback must be fresh relative to selection. The fallback is a sufficient theoretical certificate, not the frozen method.
- The sufficient size-weight dual certificate is a sufficient condition for the model-expectation objective, including at simplex boundaries, rather than a necessary condition in that generality. It has not been checked for the campaign's exact model-answer matrix. The exact-model result compares to a population target; a fixed finite empirical reference and a finite synthetic draw can have different optima.
- Support restriction assumes positive target mass on the finite support. In the target-summary radius, the independence and common-client-law hypotheses are substantive; they do not automatically follow from a benchmark partition. The finite model-only lower bound and parity example are counterexamples, not estimates of empirical performance.
- The report's bound statements and displayed proofs were otherwise retained; no numerical error was found in the two size-weight examples. Their conclusions are conditional on their stated assumptions. The population decomposition is included to preserve the original theoretical framework, without presenting its terms as measured quantities.

These are checks of mathematical examples, not experiments. Build `proofs.tex` twice with `pdflatex` from this directory to resolve references. The supplement also makes explicit that the population KL/expected-likelihood optimum and the multinomial client-label MLE do not imply finite observed-data mixture MLE; a one-observation Bernoulli example demonstrates the distinction. The definitions of the local errors used in Statement 26 are included with that statement.
